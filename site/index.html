How to read this page: terms, signs and colours

Terms

Delay
How many minutes after the timetable a vehicle arrives at a stop (a minus sign means early), from BKK's last real-time prediction before the arrival.
On time
From 1 minute early to 3 minutes late.
Delay added
How much the delay grew between the previous stop and this one. It shows where time is lost.
Stretch
The part of a route between two neighbouring stops.
Rush hour
Workdays 7–9 and 16–18, Budapest time.
Arrivals
How many stop arrivals a number is based on. More arrivals, more certain.

More terms

Typical delay
The median: half of the arrivals are better than this, half are worse.
Bad-day delay
The 95th percentile: only 1 arrival in 20 is later than this.
Extra time to plan
Bad-day delay minus typical delay: how much earlier you must leave to be on time 19 times out of 20.
Bunching
Two vehicles of the same line arriving close together, then a long gap.
City centre, middle ring, outskirts
Under 3 km, 3–8 km and over 8 km from Deák Ferenc tér.
Left out as errors
Stale predictions, trips moved to the wrong timetable slot and recurring data errors. They are not used in any figure; see 6 · About the data.

Signs and colours

Shaded bar behind a number
How big the value is compared with the other rows of the same table.
few data
Fewer than 1,000 arrivals: the number may still change a lot.
12.3 Grey number
Based on only a few arrivals: read it with care.
–
No data for that cell.
Purple and amber lines in charts
Workdays and weekends or holidays.
Map colours
makes up time 0–15 s 15–40 s 40–90 s over 90 s added
KPI
A measurable target for the project itself (data coverage, quality, speed, cost), not for BKK. See 6 · About the data.
Reliability index (1–10)
Five measures per district, each scaled between the worst (1) and the best district (10), then weighted. It compares districts with each other, not with a fixed standard.
Live road traffic (TomTom)
A switch on the delay map. It shows car traffic right now, straight from TomTom; we do not store it.
Associations, not causes
When two things go together (for example traffic lights and delay), the table does not prove that one causes the other.

2 · Where is time lost?

Places on the network where delay is added: districts, stretches between stops, stops, zones and rush-hour congestion.

Which districts have the most reliable public transport?

A reliability index for each of Budapest's 23 districts, from all arrivals at the district's stops. Five measures are each scaled from 1 (the worst district) to 10 (the best), then weighted and added up, so 10 means best in Budapest. The measures are shown too, because the weights are our choice. District boundaries: © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Which lines already start late?

Delay at the second stop of each trip (the first stop always shows the timetable until the vehicle leaves). A late start points to the terminal or the previous trip, not to traffic.

Where along the route is the most time lost?

Each row is the stretch between two stops of one line, with the average delay added on it. The first and last stretch of a trip are left out; at least 100 arrivals.

Where are buses slowest?

Measured speed of buses and trolleybuses between two stops, from BKK vehicle positions. Night lines (9xx) and the first and last stretch of a trip are left out, because vehicles wait there by timetable.

Where does rush hour slow buses down most?

Bus and trolleybus speed in workday rush hour against the same stretch at night, when the roads are empty. Top: the network average; below: the worst stretches. Day lines only; at least 10 speed readings in both.

City centre against the outskirts

City centre = under 3 km from Deák Ferenc tér, middle ring = 3–8 km, outskirts = over 8 km.

Stops with the largest average delay

Each row is one line at one stop. A stop high on this list is often late because of what happened before it, not at it.

3 · What goes with more delay?

Street features, weather, disruptions and operations compared. These are associations, not proof of cause.

Does the street matter?

Bus and trolleybus. Delay added before the stop, by street feature. The zone columns compare like with like, because busy streets have bus lanes, lights and malls all at once. Grey = under 300 arrivals. Associations, not proof of cause.

Do big transfer hubs slow buses down?

Delay added on the stretch after a stop, by how many lines serve the stop and whether it has a metro or HÉV connection. More passengers getting on and off take more time.

Do many stops slow a line down?

Lines grouped by how many stops they have per kilometre, with the delay added per kilometre. Bus and trolleybus.

Delay during BKK disruption notices

Grouped by the BKK notice active on the line at the time: works, traffic, detour, no service and so on. “none” = no notice.

Delay by weather

How much does rain really add?

Rainy hours against dry hours of the same vehicle type, day type and hour, so a rainy Monday morning is compared with a dry Monday morning, not with a quiet Sunday.

Delay by temperature

Air temperature in that hour. Most useful in winter, when frosty mornings appear.

Daylight and darkness

Delay added between stops in daylight and in the dark, bus and trolleybus. It mostly reflects the time of day (less traffic at night), not darkness itself.

Does a late trip make the next one late?

How late a trip starts, by how late the same vehicle finished its previous trip.

Does a longer break at the terminal help?

Rows: the planned break between the vehicle's previous trip and this one. Columns: how late the previous trip ended. Each cell shows how late this trip starts, in minutes: a long break should absorb a late arrival. Grey = under 50 trips.

Which lines run in bunches?

Share of arrivals that came less than half the planned gap after the previous vehicle of the same line: two buses together, then a long wait.

6 · About the data

Our project KPIs, how much data was collected, how healthy the processing is, and what was left out as errors.

Are we meeting our KPIs?

Eight project KPIs, measured by the pipeline itself. “Latest” is the most recent processed day and sets the status; “On target” counts every day of this view, so an earlier bad day stays visible. Processing time comes from the Glue job's run history, the cost from the measured run time and the data Athena reads (list prices).

Is the data processing healthy?

One row per day from the processing job. Healthy: “Not matched” near 0 %, “Left out as errors” a few percent, no warnings. “Not tracked live” = trips with exactly 0 delay at every stop.

Trips left out as data errors

Trips 30+ minutes late at every stop on two or more days. A real incident happens once; these come back day after day, so they are vehicles matched to the wrong trip. They are not used anywhere on this page.

How much data was collected each day?

One row per day. The student lab sleeps after four hours unless it is restarted, so some hours can be missing.
